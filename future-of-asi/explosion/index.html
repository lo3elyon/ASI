01 · The idea

The last invention that man need ever make

In 1965 the statistician I. J. Good wrote down a simple loop. A machine smart enough to design machines could design a smarter one, which could design a smarter one again. He called it an intelligence explosion. Scroll through how the idea grew.

03 · Takeoff speed

Fast or slow, and why it matters

Nearly everyone in this debate expects AI to speed up AI. The fight is about how sudden it is. A slow takeoff gives us years of warning signs. A fast one gives us days.

11 · The long view

Growth has been speeding up for ages

Zoom out far enough and the whole human story looks like one long take off. That is why some economists take the idea of an explosion seriously, and why others push back.

What Roodman did

In 2020 David Roodman of Open Philanthropy fitted a statistical model to gross world product from 10,000 BCE to 2019. Growth has tended to speed up as the economy grew, not stay constant.

Source

The strange result

If that pattern simply continued, his model sends output to infinity in finite time. The median year for that explosion came out as 2047, give or take 16 years.

Source

How he reads it

Roodman says it would not really reach infinity. He reads it as a sign the human system is unstable, and names AI as the most plausible channel for another burst.

Source

The pushback

Ben Garfinkel looked at the same history and found very little support for the idea that growth follows one smooth speeding up curve.

Source
12 · The key number

Can software alone run away?

Forethought asks a sharp question: once AI can do all of AI research, could better algorithms alone, on the same chips, make progress speed up and up. It comes down to one number, r.

Starting from a first doubling of 1 month after AI can do all AI research, as in the report. Each next doubling takes 2 to the power of (1/r minus 1) times as long as the one before.

r is how many times AI software doubles each time the total research effort doubles. Above 1, each doubling comes faster than the last. Below 1, progress fizzles out. Exactly 1 is steady exponential growth.

1.4Image recognition
0.8Computer chess
1.6Reinforcement learning sample efficiency
3.5SAT solvers
1.1Linear programming
about 4Algorithms in general

Their best guess for AI software today is r of about 1 to 4. With chips held fixed they lower it to about 0.5 to 2. Software efficiency has been doubling about every 6 months.

Daniel Eth and Tom Davidson, Will AI R&D Automation Cause a Software Intelligence Explosion?, Forethought, 26 March 2025. Read the report

15 · What labs promise

The labs' own tripwires

The biggest labs now say in writing that AI doing AI research is a danger sign. Here is what two of them committed to, in their own documents.

OpenAI Preparedness Framework, version 2 (April 2025)

AI self-improvement is a Tracked Category

OpenAI split self-improvement out on its own because it can mean a hard to track, rapid speed up in AI capabilities. Its High level is reached when a model's impact is like giving every OpenAI researcher a highly capable mid-career research engineer assistant, compared with 2024. Reaching it requires High standard security controls.

Source
Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy (update of 10 February 2026)

AI R&D-4 needs an affirmative safety case

Once a model crosses the AI R&D-4 capability threshold, Anthropic says it must write an affirmative case naming the most immediate misalignment risks from models pursuing misaligned goals, and explain how it has reduced them.

Source

These are company policies, not laws. Companies can change them, and outside groups argue about whether they are strong enough.

18 · If it starts

Staying in control of a faster mind

If AI starts doing most AI research, people need ways to stay safe even when they cannot fully trust the model. Two efforts show the hope and the difficulty.

AI control

Ryan Greenblatt, Buck Shlegeris, Kshitij Sachan and Fabien Roger (Redwood Research), December 2023

They build safety protocols that should hold even if the model is trying to get around them on purpose. One idea is to have a weaker model you trust watch a stronger model you do not, and send the most suspicious work to people.

Source

Superalignment

OpenAI, July 2023 to May 2024

OpenAI set out to solve how to align superintelligent systems within four years and promised 20% of its computing power. Employees later reported the compute never came, and the team's co-lead Jan Leike resigned in May 2024.

20 · No takeover needed

Losing control without a single bang

Not every bad ending looks like an explosion. A 2025 paper argues people could slowly lose their grip as AI takes over the work that keeps the economy, culture and states tied to human needs.

  1. 1

    Economy

    As AI replaces human labour, firms and markets depend less on people, and what people want carries less weight.

  2. 2

    Culture

    More of what we read and watch is made by AI for AI driven systems, and human voices count for less.

  3. 3

    States

    Governments that need fewer citizens to work, fight or pay tax have weaker reasons to listen to them.

  4. 4

    Together

    Each system weakens the others' ties to people, so the effects add up. The authors call this an existential risk that needs no rogue AI at all.

Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger and David Duvenaud, Gradual Disempowerment, arXiv 2501.16946, January 2025. Read the paper
