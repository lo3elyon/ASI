01 · The idea

The last invention we need make

In 1965 the statistician I. J. Good wrote down a simple loop. A machine smart enough to design machines could design a smarter one, which could design a smarter one again. He called it an intelligence explosion. Scroll through how the idea grew.

02 · The loop

Run the recursive loop

Better AI does more AI research. More research makes better AI. Whether this loop fizzles or explodes depends on a few numbers nobody knows yet. Move the sliders and watch the curve.

Returns to research. Above 1, each improvement makes the next one easier, so the loop runs away. Below 1, ideas get harder to find faster than AI helps.

03 · Takeoff speed

Fast or slow, and why it matters

Nearly everyone in this debate expects AI to speed up AI. The fight is about how sudden it is. A slow takeoff gives us years of warning signs. A fast one gives us days.

08 · Agent thinking, agent wanting

Inside an agent's mind

Give an agent almost any goal and the same helper goals grow out of it: stay running, keep the goal, get resources, get smarter. Researchers call this instrumental convergence. Pick a goal and watch the tree grow.
