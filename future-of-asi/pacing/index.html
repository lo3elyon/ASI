Many researchers also consider recursive self-improvement (RSI) plausible within the next few years, accelerating progress in a way that could outpace our ability to understand and govern these systems. Deliberate pacing is a heavy-handed and potentially extreme measure, and we may never need it. But if we do, it cannot be safely invented in the middle of a crisis. It is very difficult to design well, and if done poorly could do more harm than good. Any successful coordination framework must be evidence-based, avoiding arbitrary thresholds that fail to capture true risk while stifling innovation, and instead be grounded in rigorous, measurable assessments of risk. Like any critical infrastructure, it must be researched, tested, and built before it is needed. This is why we must carefully design it now, rather than waiting. Such a capability must be international and measurable: international because no single lab or country can pace a frontier everyone else is racing toward; measurable because effective governance requires rigorous, transparent, and verifiable evaluations of frontier AI systems. We do not know if, or when, deliberate pacing will become necessary. But building the capacity now, including technical and governance tools and an international cooperation framework, gives society valuable optionality. To be effective, this needs to exist before the decisive moment arrives. At the speed the frontier is moving, that moment may arrive sooner than we expect. Building the capacity is not the same as deciding when, or whether, to use it. The latter requires great caution, rigorous evidence, and broad international consensus. But without tools in place, there will be no choice at all. Capabilities cannot be reliably built during a crisis. Preparing before a crisis is the prudent path.
