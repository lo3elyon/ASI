How I read p(doom) numbers without panicking

When someone says 20% or 90%, what does that actually mean, and what should you do with it?

The first time I saw a list of p(doom) numbers I felt cold. Ten percent. Twenty percent. Ninety percent. These were not random people. These were researchers I respect.

After a while I learned to read them more calmly. Here is how.

It is a judgment, not a measurement

p(doom) is shorthand for someone's personal probability that AI leads to a catastrophe like extinction or permanent loss of control. There is no lab instrument that measures it. It is a summary of a person's whole view of the future squeezed into one number.

That means two things. The number tells you how worried that person is. It does not tell you the true chance.

Look at what they mean by doom

People mean different things. Some mean extinction. Some mean humans losing control of the future even if we survive. Some give a number for this century, some for the next decade. Always check the source. On this site every p(doom) badge links to where it came from.

Look at the spread

The striking thing is not any one number. It is the range. Some serious people say under one percent. Others say over ninety. When experts disagree that much, it means the problem is genuinely uncertain. Uncertainty is not comfort, but it is also not fate.

Ask how they got there

I like Joe Carlsmith's approach. He broke the risk into a chain of steps: will we build very capable agentic AI, will there be strong incentives to deploy it, will alignment be hard, will misaligned systems seek power, will that scale to disempowerment. He multiplied his guesses and got about 5% by 2070, and later said his estimate had gone above 10%.

You can do the same with the p(doom) calculator in Tools. Put in your own numbers. You will see which step matters most to you.

What to do with the feeling

If any of these numbers is even roughly right, AI risk is one of the biggest problems of our time. The right response is not panic. It is attention and action. Pick one thing from the Prepare page and do it this week.
