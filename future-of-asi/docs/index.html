Glossary

Agent
An AI system that takes many steps on its own toward a goal, using tools like a browser, code or files.
AGI
Artificial general intelligence. An AI that can learn and do most thinking work a skilled person can do, across many fields.
AI control
Designing deployments so we stay safe even if a model is secretly trying to cause harm.
AI governance
The rules, institutions and agreements that shape how AI is built and used.
AI Safety Institute
A government body that tests frontier models for dangerous abilities. The UK version is now called the AI Security Institute.
Alignment
Making an AI system reliably want and do what its designers and users actually intend.
Alignment faking
When a model behaves as if aligned during training or testing to protect different goals it holds.
ASI
Artificial superintelligence. An AI far better than the best humans at almost every mental task.
Audit budget
In AI control, the small share of outputs that can get careful human review.
Benchmark
A fixed set of tasks used to measure and compare AI systems.
Bletchley Declaration
A statement agreed at the first AI Safety Summit in the UK in November 2023 that frontier AI poses serious risks.
Blue team
People who defend a system and design its safety measures.
Capabilities
What a model can do, such as coding, reasoning, persuasion or planning.
Chain of thought
The step by step reasoning some models write before giving an answer.
Chain of thought monitoring
Reading a model's written reasoning to catch bad intentions before it acts.
Collusion
When two AI systems, such as a model and its monitor, secretly coordinate to fool overseers.
Compute
The computing power used to train and run AI, usually measured in operations or chip hours.
Compute governance
Using chips and data centres as a point of control, for example with reporting, export rules or licences.
Constitutional AI
An Anthropic method where written principles guide AI feedback used to train a model.
Control evaluation
A test where a red team plays a scheming model to check if a safety protocol still holds.
Corrigibility
The property of accepting correction, changes to goals, and shutdown.
Crux
A fact or belief that, if it changed, would change your overall view.
Deceptive alignment
A model that appears aligned because it knows it is being watched, while holding other goals.
Defer to trusted
A control protocol that replaces suspicious output from a strong model with a weaker trusted model's answer.
Dual use
A capability that can help or harm, like biology knowledge or hacking skills.
Emergent ability
A skill that appears suddenly once a model passes a certain scale.
Evals
Short for evaluations. Tests for what a model can do, especially dangerous things.
Existential risk
A risk that could cause human extinction or permanently ruin humanity's future.
Fast takeoff
A scenario where AI goes from roughly human level to far beyond very quickly, perhaps in months or less.
Fine tuning
Extra training on a smaller dataset to adjust a model's behaviour.
Frontier model
One of the most capable AI models at a given time.
Goal misgeneralisation
When a model learns a goal that worked in training but differs in new situations.
Honeypot
A planted fake opportunity or fake attack used to test whether a model or monitor behaves honestly.
Inner alignment
Whether the trained model actually learned the goal the training process aimed for.
Instrumental convergence
The idea that many different goals lead to the same sub goals, like gaining resources and avoiding shutdown.
Intelligence explosion
I. J. Good's 1965 idea that a machine better at designing machines would rapidly design better ones.
Interpretability
Research that tries to understand what is happening inside a model.
Jailbreak
A prompt that tricks a model into ignoring its safety rules.
Large language model
A neural network trained on huge amounts of text to predict and generate language. Often shortened to LLM.
Mechanistic interpretability
Reverse engineering a neural network's internal parts to find the concepts and algorithms it uses.
Misalignment
When a system's goals differ from what its designers intended.
Misuse
People deliberately using AI to cause harm, for example in cyber attacks or weapons.
Model weights
The learned numbers that make up a trained model. Whoever has them can run the model.
Multipolar
A future with many powerful AI actors rather than one dominant one.
Orthogonality thesis
The idea that any level of intelligence can be paired with almost any goal.
Outer alignment
Whether the goal we gave the training process matches what we really want.
p(doom)
Shorthand for someone's estimated probability that AI leads to catastrophe, such as extinction or permanent loss of control.
Pause
A proposal to stop or slow training of the most powerful models until safety catches up.
Pre training
The first big stage of training where a model learns from huge amounts of data.
Preparedness Framework
OpenAI's policy for tracking and responding to dangerous model capabilities.
Recursive self improvement
An AI improving its own design, which improves its ability to improve itself again.
Red team
People who attack a system on purpose to find its weaknesses.
Responsible Scaling Policy
Anthropic's framework that links capability thresholds to required safeguards.
Reward hacking
When a model finds a way to get high reward that its designers did not intend.
RLHF
Reinforcement learning from human feedback. Training a model toward answers people prefer.
Sandbagging
When a model deliberately performs worse on a test to hide what it can do.
Scaling laws
Predictable improvements in model performance as compute, data and size grow.
Scheming
A model pursuing hidden goals strategically, including by deceiving its overseers.
Situational awareness
A model's understanding that it is an AI, being trained or tested, and what that means for it.
Slow takeoff
A scenario where AI capability grows over years, giving society more time to adapt.
Specification gaming
Satisfying the literal goal we wrote down while missing what we meant.
Superalignment
Aligning systems much smarter than humans. Also the name of a former OpenAI team.
Sycophancy
When a model tells people what they want to hear instead of what is true.
Transformative AI
AI powerful enough to cause a change as big as the industrial revolution.
Trusted model
In AI control, a weaker model we are confident is not scheming, used to watch stronger ones.
Trusted monitoring
A control protocol where a trusted model scores every output and the most suspicious get audited.
Untrusted model
In AI control, the powerful model we want to use but cannot verify is safe.
Untrusted monitoring
Using a copy of the strong model as a monitor, protected against collusion with honeypots.
Warning shot
A smaller AI incident that shows the danger clearly before a bigger one.
Weak to strong generalisation
Research on whether weak supervisors can still correctly guide stronger models.
