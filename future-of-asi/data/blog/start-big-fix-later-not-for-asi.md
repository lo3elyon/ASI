My motto is simple. Start big, fix later, keep shipping. It is how I built every project in this repo. Ship something ambitious, see what breaks, fix it, ship again.

It works because the cost of a mistake is small. A broken button, a wrong colour, a slow page. Someone tells me, I fix it, nobody gets hurt.

## Why it does not work for ASI

With superintelligence, "fix later" might not exist. If we build a system much smarter than us and its goals are wrong, it may not let us fix it. It may not even let us notice. That is the core of the alignment problem: we might only get one real try.

Eliezer Yudkowsky and others have made this point for years. You can iterate on a rocket because a failed rocket does not stop you from building the next one. A failed superintelligence might.

## What that means in practice

It means the parts of AI work that matter most should feel more like aircraft safety than like a hackathon.

1. Test before you ship, especially for dangerous abilities.
2. Assume your system might be hiding something, and design control around that.
3. Make it possible to stop. Shutdown buttons, monitoring, and rules that actually bind.
4. Coordinate with others so nobody has to choose between safety and survival in a race.

## What I keep from my motto

I still think "keep shipping" is the right spirit for safety work itself. Ship research, ship explainers, ship tools, ship policy proposals. The field needs more builders, not fewer.

So here is my updated motto. Start big, fix later, keep shipping, for everything except the thing that cannot be fixed later. For that one, we go slow, together, and we get it right.
