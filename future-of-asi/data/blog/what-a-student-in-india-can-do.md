For a long time I thought AI safety was something that happened far away, in big offices with people who went to famous universities. I was wrong. A lot of the field is open, online and hungry for new people.

Here is what I would tell any student in India, including myself two years ago.

## Learn the ideas first

Before any code, understand the problem. Do a structured course. **BlueDot Impact** runs free courses on AI safety and AI governance with small discussion groups. The Alignment 101 course on this site is a gentle start you can finish in an evening or two.

Read slowly. Write notes in your own words. If you can explain alignment, AI control and evaluations to a friend, you are already ahead of most people.

## Build small things in public

This is where students actually have an advantage. We have time and we are used to hackathons.

1. Reproduce a simple interpretability result on a small model and write up what you saw.
2. Build a tiny evaluation that tests a model for something specific, like sycophancy.
3. Make an explainer, a video or a visual like the ones in this repo.
4. Contribute to open source safety tools.

Post it on GitHub and write about it on DEV or LinkedIn. People in the field read these. That is how you get noticed without connections.

## Use the programs that exist

There are fellowships and programs that support people from anywhere, like **MATS** for research mentorship, and career advice from **80,000 Hours**. Many accept remote applicants. Deadlines move, so check the Resources list and the official sites.

## India matters here

India has one of the biggest pools of engineers in the world and is becoming a big AI market. Our government hosted an AI Impact Summit. Indian voices in AI policy and safety are still rare. That is a gap, and gaps are opportunities.

## Do not burn out

Take care of yourself. Sleep, eat, call home. This is a long problem, and the world needs you to last.
