Imagine the engineers who designed the first cars going on TV to say cars might wipe out humanity. You would pay attention. That is roughly what has happened with AI.

## The pioneers changed their minds

Geoffrey Hinton spent decades making neural networks work. In 2023 he left Google so he could speak openly about the dangers. Since then he has said many times that AI could become smarter than us and that we do not know how to control something smarter than us.

Yoshua Bengio, another deep learning pioneer, moved his research toward safety and now speaks widely about catastrophic risks. He chaired the International AI Safety Report, written by experts from many countries.

These are not people who want attention. They already had all the respect a scientist can get.

## What they are actually worried about

When I read their interviews closely, the worries are very specific.

1. **We do not understand what we build.** Models are trained, not written. We can test what they do, but we cannot yet read what they want.
2. **Smarter things tend to win.** In every contest between a smarter and a less smart agent, the smarter one usually gets its way.
3. **Race pressure.** Companies and countries feel they cannot slow down because someone else will not.
4. **Misuse.** Even an obedient AI can help a bad person with cyber attacks or biology.

## Not everyone agrees

Some brilliant people think these fears are exaggerated. Yann LeCun, who shared the Turing Award with Hinton and Bengio, thinks current AI is far from human level and that we will build safety as we go. I include people like him on the Researchers page too, because you should hear both sides.

What I notice is that even the skeptics usually agree on one thing: it is worth doing safety research now. The disagreement is mostly about how soon and how hard.

## How to read their words

Every quote on this site has a link to where it was said. Please click them. Read the whole interview. Do not trust a screenshot, including mine. That habit of checking is one of the most useful things you can bring to this topic.
