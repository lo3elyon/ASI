A site full of warnings can start to feel like an echo chamber. So here are the strongest reasons I have found for thinking things might go well. I find some of them convincing.

## Progress might slow

Scaling needs huge amounts of compute, energy and data. Each step costs much more than the last. Some researchers think current methods will hit limits well before superintelligence, which would give us more time.

## Alignment might be easier than feared

Today's models are surprisingly good at following human values in normal situations. Techniques like RLHF and constitutions worked better than many people expected. Interpretability keeps finding real structure inside models. Maybe the hard version of the problem never shows up.

## We can use AI to help

If early powerful AI can be controlled, it can help us with alignment research itself. This is the whole bet behind AI control and many lab safety plans. Humans with AI helpers might move much faster on safety than humans alone.

## Warning shots might wake us up

Before any world ending event, we will probably see smaller failures. Those could push governments and labs to act, the way accidents improved aviation safety.

## Institutions can adapt

We have managed other dangerous technologies, like nuclear weapons, without a global catastrophe so far. It was messy and partly luck, but treaties and norms did form.

## Why I still take the risk seriously

Every one of these arguments says "might". None of them says "will". When the downside is losing everything, "might be fine" is not enough. I want us to hope for the best and prepare for the worst at the same time.

That is exactly why the Futures page includes Flourishing as a real branch. It is reachable. We just have to choose it.
