I am a final year computer science student. I build AI agents for hackathons, I test every new model the day it comes out, and I post AI news almost every day. So I am not scared of AI. I love it.

But over the last two years something kept bothering me. The people warning loudest about AI were not random people on the internet. They were the people who built it. Geoffrey Hinton left Google so he could talk freely about the risks. Yoshua Bengio, one of the three people who won the Turing Award for deep learning, changed his whole research focus to safety. The heads of the biggest labs signed a statement saying extinction risk from AI should be a global priority.

When the people who know the most are worried, I think the rest of us should at least listen.

## The problem I saw

The information was everywhere and nowhere. A quote in one podcast, a number in a tweet, a paper on arXiv, a two hour interview on YouTube. Most of my friends had never heard of AI control or alignment. When they did hear about it, it was usually as a scary headline with no source.

So I wanted to build one place that does three things.

1. **Shows the warnings honestly.** Real people, real words, and a link to where they said it. No made up quotes.
2. **Explains the ideas simply.** If my younger cousin cannot follow it, I rewrite it.
3. **Gives you something to do.** A course, a checklist, tools to think with. Not just fear.

## Why the name

A threshold is a doorway. You stand on it for a moment, and then you are in a different room. I think that is where we are with AI. We are standing on the line between a world where humans are the smartest thing on the planet and a world where we might not be.

You cannot stay on a threshold forever. But you can choose how you walk through it.

## What is inside

There is a scenario explorer where you can push sliders and watch different futures get more or less likely. There is a directory of researchers with their p(doom) numbers, warnings and videos. There is a timeline of warnings going back to Alan Turing in 1951. There is a library of verified videos, a playable AI control game, a free course with a certificate, a preparation page, tools and docs.

I built it the way I build everything: start big, fix later, keep shipping. If you find a mistake, tell me. That is how it gets better.
