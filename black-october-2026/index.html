The name

"Black October" is a nickname for one very loud week of bad news.

It is a wave of disclosures, not one big attack.

Between 19 and 22 October 2026, a lot of serious security problems became public at almost the same time: two unauthenticated PHP flaws (PHP2Shell), eight eMudhra certificate keys anyone could work out, a poisoned LiteLLM release, a CVSS 10.0 GitLab bug, and breaches at the hosting platforms Railway and Zeabur.

On 22 October an X account called AISafetyMemes posted a list of about 27 items and called the previous 48 hours "the worst in cybersecurity history". The name stuck. CNBC used it on 23 October, and former CISA Director Chris Krebs told CNBC: "This is a significant week or two in cybersecurity… 3% of all critical vulnerabilities ever discovered have been found in the last month."

The important part: most of these attacks were not new. They had been running for weeks or months before anyone said so. The ETS breach ran from 22 May to 18 September. AOMEI's update servers were tampered with in May and July. PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 was exploited from May or June. So the clock in "48 hours" is the disclosure clock, not the attack clock.

Not everyone agrees

SecurityToday makes the point that many of these attacks had been running for months, so the label describes a disclosure wave. Forkast searched X on 21–23 October, could not find the original post, and says the label "hides the clock". The claim that this was "the worst 48 hours in cybersecurity history" is not backed by any measurement, and the viral claim about "AI agent swarms hacking half the internet" has no source behind it.

    What's not certain.

    Read these facts with care

    • The "worst 48 hours in cybersecurity history" claim is not supported by any measurement. Forkast searched X on 21–23 Oct and could not find the original post.
    • Entry points were never published for Capcom, Quest Diagnostics, Railway, Ahold Delhaize, GMO Onamae, Bullhorn, the French Danfoss attack, the West Bengal State Data Centre, ConnectWise, AOMEI, LiteLLM's pipeline and the SBI dataset's origin.
    • Ransomware victim counts disagree sharply between trackers (HackerFeeds 813, Ransomnews 823, Invaders.ie 274 for the same month). All are leak-site claims, not verified incidents.
    • Anthropic's IPO figures come from reported drafts (WSJ, Reuters); there was no public S-1 on EDGAR as of 21 Oct.
    • OpenAI's $30B round and Amazon–Decart are reported talks, not closed deals. Stripe–OpenRouter's timing and $7.5B price were never confirmed by the companies.
    • Dates are the event or disclosure date as the sources report them, mostly US/EU time.

    Glossary.

    RCE
    Remote code execution: an attacker runs their own commands on your machine over the internet.
    Zero-day
    A flaw attackers use before a fix exists, so defenders have had zero days to patch.
    Supply-chain attack
    Poisoning something you trust and install, such as a package, update or plugin, instead of attacking you directly.
    CA private key
    The secret a certificate authority signs website certificates with; whoever holds it can fake HTTPS for any site.
    Prompt injection
    Hidden instructions in a page or email that an AI reads and obeys as if you gave them.
    Ransomware
    Malware that locks or steals data, then demands payment to unlock it or keep it private.
    Environment variables
    Settings an app reads at startup, often holding API keys and database passwords.
    Non-human identity
    A login used by an app, bot or service account rather than a person, often without MFA.
