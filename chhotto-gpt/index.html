No PyTorch. No TensorFlow. No API. Just ~275 lines of hand-written JavaScript: a tensor autograd engine, multi-head causal attention, AdamW and a GPT, all checked against numerical gradients. ৩. কবিতা লেখাও Make it write

Give it the start of a line and let it continue. Low temperature = safe and repetitive; high = wild and inventive. It has only ever read one book, so treat it like a very small, very devoted student of Tagore.

৪. নিজের লেখা দিয়ে শেখাও Train it on your own text

Paste anything: your DEV posts, a WhatsApp export, Nazrul, Jibanananda, code, English. The vocabulary is rebuilt from your text, the model is re-initialised, and training starts from zero. Nothing leaves your browser.

৫. কীভাবে কাজ করে How it works

The architecture

characters → ids (vocab ≈86)
↓
token embedding (64) + position embedding (32×64)
↓
Block × 2
LayerNorm → 4-head causal self-attention → residual
LayerNorm → MLP 64→256→64 (GELU) → residual
↓
final LayerNorm → linear head → softmax over characters
↓
sample the next character, append, repeat

Why characters, not words?

Bengali has ~50 base letters plus কার-চিহ্ন, হসন্ত and যুক্তাক্ষর (conjuncts). Learning at the Unicode code-point level means the model has to discover for itself that ্ glues consonants together and that া follows a consonant, which is exactly what you see happen in the timeline.

Is the gradient really right?

Yes. gradcheck.js perturbs random weights in every tensor and compares the analytic gradient with a finite difference. Worst relative error at build time: 0.0017.

How big is this compared with real LLMs?

About 113 thousand parameters. GPT-2 small has 124 million (≈1,100× more); frontier models have hundreds of billions to trillions. Same idea, same maths, a few million times less compute.

The corpus

Rabindranath Tagore, Gitanjali (1910/1913 Bengali edition), public domain, from Bengali Wikisource. 92% train / 8% validation split.
